Klarna

At Klarna, I worked on credit strategy, experimentation and market expansion across European consumer lending portfolios. My role combined portfolio analytics, underwriting strategy, third-party data optimisation and the design of new credit policies.

Credit Strategy Optimisation

Problem

The portfolio contained underwriting inefficiencies that were contributing to avoidable credit losses and limiting the effectiveness of the existing decision strategy.

Approach

I led analysis to identify where the underwriting strategy was underperforming and translated the findings into practical credit-policy changes.

The work included:

Changes to underwriting rules

Credit-limit adjustments

Introduction and refinement of credit models

Portfolio monitoring to understand the effect of strategy changes

Balancing loss reduction against approval rates and annual commercial targets

Business impact

The strategy changes contributed to an approximately 20% reduction in credit losses.

The work was not simply focused on tightening credit policy. The objective was to improve portfolio performance while continuing to support the wider business targets, requiring careful trade-offs between risk appetite, customer approvals and commercial growth.

Experimentation and Credit Bureau Strategy

Problem

Third-party credit-bureau data represented both an important underwriting input and a significant recurring cost. The existing bureau strategy could therefore be improved on two dimensions: decision quality and cost efficiency.

Approach

I introduced champion-challenger and A/B testing approaches to evaluate alternative third-party data strategies.

For the German market, I designed and analysed an experiment testing the order in which primary and secondary credit bureaus were called. The objective was to determine whether a different ordering could preserve or improve underwriting performance while reducing unnecessary bureau queries.

Business impact

The resulting strategy:

Improved approval outcomes

Reduced credit losses

Generated approximately £150,000 per month in credit-bureau savings

Equivalent to approximately £1.8 million in annualised savings

This was a strong example of experimentation producing benefits across multiple dimensions simultaneously: lower operating cost, better customer outcomes and improved credit performance.

Why it mattered

Rather than treating third-party bureau configuration as a fixed operational process, I treated it as an optimisation problem that could be tested empirically.

The champion-challenger framework created a repeatable way to evaluate whether additional external data genuinely justified its cost and whether changes to the decision waterfall improved overall portfolio economics.

Switzerland Market Launch

Problem

Klarna required an initial credit strategy for launching into Switzerland. A new market requires risk policies that are commercially viable while accounting for differences in available data, customer behaviour and local market characteristics.

Approach

I designed, built and rolled out the initial go-to-market credit strategy for Switzerland.

This involved translating Klarna's broader credit-risk framework into a practical launch strategy, including the initial underwriting approach and decision policies required to begin lending safely in the new market.

Business impact

The strategy enabled the business to launch with a functioning credit-risk framework from day one, supporting market expansion while establishing appropriate underwriting controls.

I received an Employee of the Month nomination for my contribution to the project.

Why it mattered

The project required more than model development or portfolio analysis. It involved making decisions with limited local performance history, balancing risk and growth, and building a workable strategy under the uncertainty that comes with launching a new market.
