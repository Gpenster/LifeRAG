Thin-File Credit Scorecard

Problem

A fintech client was seeing weaker credit-risk performance for younger customers. The initial assumption was that age itself should define a separate modelling segment, but I found that the underlying issue was more closely related to the depth and maturity of customers' credit histories.

Approach

Rather than creating a scorecard simply for customers below a particular age, I reframed the segment around "thin-file" characteristics. The population was defined using indicators such as:

Less than 18 months of credit-card information

Less than two years of recent electoral-roll history

Limited depth of established credit information

I then developed a dedicated scorecard for this population.

Business impact

The new segmentation produced a meaningful uplift in predictive performance compared with applying the standard scorecard to these customers. The resulting Gini remained lower than for a mature-credit-file population, which was expected given the limited behavioural information available, but the dedicated approach materially improved the lender's ability to differentiate risk within an otherwise poorly scored segment.

Why it mattered

The key insight was that the modelling problem was not fundamentally "young customers"; it was customers with limited observable credit history. Reframing the problem in this way created a more defensible and reusable risk segmentation.

Subprime Credit Scorecard

Problem

I developed a scorecard for a subprime lender operating in a particularly high-risk population. Approximately 10% of the development sample defaulted, making effective risk differentiation commercially important.

Approach

I developed the scorecard using a combination of bureau and customer characteristics, including:

Age

Current-account information

CATO data

Credit-card information

Housing data

The modelling work focused on identifying variables that could reliably separate better- and worse-performing customers despite the high underlying default rate.

Business impact

The final scorecard achieved a Gini coefficient of approximately 45%, a level the client considered successful for this high-risk population.

The model gave the lender a stronger basis for differentiating applicants within a segment where the overall probability of poor performance was already high, supporting better underwriting and pricing decisions.

Single Customer View

Problem

Financial institutions can hold multiple accounts or portfolios belonging to the same underlying customer. Without a reliable method of linking these records, organisations may struggle to understand total customer exposure and satisfy customer-aggregation and FSCS-related requirements.

Solution

I developed a Single Customer View solution that identified the same customer across multiple portfolios using credit-bureau information including:

Name

Address

Account information

The solution used these attributes to link records and create a consolidated view of the customer across otherwise separate portfolios.

Business impact

The product initially generated approximately £40,000 in monthly revenue, equivalent to roughly £480,000 annualised, while also helping clients address customer aggregation and FSCS-related requirements.

The work led to an Employee of the Month award.

Creditworthiness Assessment Bill

Context

I supported analytical work relating to Lord Bird's proposal to include rental-payment information in consumer credit assessments.

Contribution

I provided evidence and analysis on how rental data could contribute to creditworthiness assessment, helping inform the discussion around whether regular rental payments could provide useful additional information when evaluating consumers.

Impact

The work contributed analytical evidence to a live public-policy discussion about broadening the information available for credit assessment.
