# Leadership

I see myself primarily as a strong individual contributor who can also lead teams and shape how work gets done. My leadership style is highly delivery-focused: I care about producing measurable business impact, making decisions objectively, and creating enough structure for people to execute effectively.

I am most effective in roles where I can stay close to the technical and analytical work while also setting direction, prioritising problems and helping teams focus on the work that matters most.

## Building the Risk Analytics and Data Science Capability at Pliant

### Context

At Pliant, I lead credit risk strategy and AI and was tasked with building out the risk analytics and data-science capability.

The organisation was still developing its data culture, so part of the challenge was not simply hiring people, but also creating a more objective and evidence-led way of working.

### Approach

I focused on:

- Hiring the first members of the risk analytics and data-science team
- Prioritising a small number of high-value problems rather than building capability in isolation
- Getting new hires contributing to meaningful projects quickly
- Bringing more structure and objectivity into risk decision-making
- Staying close to delivery while setting the direction for the team

### Impact

I successfully completed the first hires and got the team working on commercially relevant, high-impact projects quickly.

The broader objective has been to move risk decisions away from subjective judgement where possible and toward measurable, data-driven decision-making.

### Leadership philosophy

My approach is not to build large teams or processes for their own sake. I prefer small, capable teams with clear ownership, strong technical standards and a direct link between the work being done and the business outcome it is intended to improve.

## Leadership Team Contribution at Stenn

### Context

At Stenn, I was part of the leadership team and contributed to how different functions translated strategic priorities into operational goals.

### Approach

### Impact

## Cross-Functional Anomaly Detection Workshop

### Problem

Risk events often sit across organisational boundaries. Fraud, credit, compliance and anti-financial-crime teams may each see a different part of the same underlying behaviour, which makes it difficult to identify broader patterns if teams work independently.

### Approach

I organised a cross-functional anomaly-detection workshop bringing together:

- Compliance
- Anti-Financial Crime (AFC)
- Credit Risk
- Fraud

The session used recent cases as practical examples. Teams reviewed what had happened, identified signals that might have been visible earlier and discussed possible analytical or process solutions.

I also invited an external speaker to demonstrate and discuss tools available in the market, giving the teams an external benchmark against which to compare our internal capabilities.

### Impact

The workshop created a shared view of anomaly-detection problems across functions and helped break down siloed ownership of risk signals.

It also shifted the discussion from individual historical cases toward repeatable detection approaches and potential solutions that could be implemented across multiple risk teams.

## How I Lead

The themes that best describe my leadership style are:

- Delivery first: I value tangible outcomes over activity.
- Objective decision-making: I prefer evidence, data and measurable results wherever possible.
- Hands-on leadership: I stay close enough to the work to understand the technical detail and challenge assumptions.
- Small, capable teams: I prefer clear ownership and strong people over unnecessary layers of management.
- Cross-functional problem solving: I am comfortable bringing different functions together around a shared problem.
- 0-to-1 environments: I am particularly comfortable when the team, process or solution still needs to be created.
